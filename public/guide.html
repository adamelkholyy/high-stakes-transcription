Reviewer's Guide

Checking an AI transcript, the fast way

This tool takes a recorded interview, has speech-recognition AI produce a first-draft transcript, and then helps you — the reviewer — find and fix the parts the machine most likely got wrong, while listening against the audio.

🧪
About this online demo. You're looking at a public demonstration on a test server, loaded with one sample interview so you can try everything without any setup. The AI features here run on the project's cloud service (not on your own computer), so please explore with the built-in sample and don't type real, sensitive case details. Audio-to-text transcription and microphone recording are switched off in this demo — everything else is live.

The centre column · part 1

Reading the highlights

The colours tell you where to look first. They flag where the speech-recognition was shaky — not where it's definitely wrong. Nothing is coloured when the AI was confident.

Word-level marks

WITNESS [1:24] He said the Verrastro was parked near the junction before the car left.
Plain — the AI was confident. Most of the transcript looks like this. Amber, dotted underline — medium risk. Some uncertainty; worth a glance. Red, solid underline — high risk. The models disagreed most here; check first.

Hover any marked word for a one-line tip (e.g. “High uncertainty — click to inspect”). A high-risk line also shows a small red dot next to the speaker name.

Whole-sentence tint

Some versions shade the entire sentence instead of (or as well as) single words — useful when a whole line is doubtful, or when a line is important regardless of confidence.

SUSPECT [3:02]
I was at home the whole evening, I never went out.
We took a taxi to the station at about ten.
Plain lines stay uncoloured.

The “Sentences” switch. In sentence-capable versions a small control in the transcript header lets you choose what drives the tint:

SentencesConfidenceImportanceBoth
  • Confidence — tint by how sure the speech-recognition was (red = likely mis-heard).
  • Importance — an AI ranks which lines carry the most case-relevant content and highlights those.
  • Both — red = likely mis-transcribed and important (check first); amber = only one of the two signals fired.
⚑
If the Conflicts tool (see below) flags a line, that line also gets an amber tint in the transcript, so contradictions surface where you're reading.

The centre column · part 2

Making corrections & verifying

Fixing a word

  1. Click the word. A small popup opens with the alternatives the different AI models proposed, plus a box to type your own correction.
  2. Pick a candidate or type the right word, then Apply — or, if the word is a hallucination that was never said, press Delete (hallucination).
  3. The change is recorded instantly in the right-hand log, tagged with your name and the time.
After you edit a word
…parked near the junctionjunkshun before…

Original in grey strikethrough, your correction in blue. The line/segment also gets an ✎ edited tag.

After you remove a word
…he said the the car was red…

A removed word is struck through with a small ✕ badge. Click it again to restore.

Rewriting a whole line

To fix more than one word at once, double-click the line (or use its pencil button) and retype the sentence. The rewrite shows as a blue block:

We took a taxi to the station at about ten. rewritten
👁
Clean view vs. Show changes. By default you see your edits marked up (tracked-changes style). A toggle switches to a clean view showing only the final, corrected text — handy for reading the result as it will be filed.

Verifying a line

Verifying is how you say “I've listened to this and it's right.” Select a line and press V (or Shift+V to verify and jump to the next). Verified lines turn a calm green and the button reads:

OFFICER ✓ Verified Can you tell me where you were on the night of the fourteenth?

There's no separate “done” list — the right-hand panel simply counts how many lines you've verified out of the total, and how many high-risk lines are still outstanding.

The right column

The review panel

A narrow panel on the right that keeps score and holds your exports. It has three parts.

1 · Progress

Verified 6 / 24 segments, with a thin green bar. Below it, “3 high-risk left” (red until you've cleared them, then “high-risk done”).

2 · Recent changes

A live log of everything you do — every edit, removal and verification — newest first, each with your name, the time, the location and an optional reason.

3 · Export

A collapsible section (see the next part) with every download: the report, the corrected transcript, and the logs.

Reading the change log

Edited — junction → junkshun Removed — the → (deleted) Verified — segment checked Un-verified — undone Speaker / Split / Merge — line changes

This log is your audit trail — a full, timestamped record of who changed what, and why. It's exactly what goes into the report and the audit-trail files below.

The left column

The AI tools

Available in the AI toolkit and Complete versions. They help you navigate and cross-check — every result jumps the audio to the right moment when you click it. All AI-suggested, all to be verified.

🔍 Find

Search for a name, object or topic. It finds exact matches and close variants instantly, then an AI adds lines that are about your query even when they don't use the same words. You type the term and press Find; each hit shows a snippet and jumps the audio.

💬 Assistant

Ask a plain question about the interview — “was a weapon mentioned?”, “who paid for the taxi?” The answer cites the exact lines it drew from; click a citation to hear it. Answers are for orientation only and are never part of the record.

⚠️ Conflicts

Runs automatically and flags pairs of statements that appear to contradict each other (time, place, person or claim). Each card has two “jump” buttons — one per side — and both lines are tinted amber in the transcript. Listen to both before deciding.

🕑 Timeline

Runs automatically and lists the concrete events described in the recording, in order, with any spoken time (“around ten o'clock”). Click an event to jump straight to that moment.

📑 Outline

Opens a full-screen storyboard that breaks a long recording into chapters — an overview paragraph and a chain of “part” cards you can jump into. Good for getting the shape of a long interview quickly.

A note on “Importance”

There's no separate button for it — the Importance setting on the Sentences switch (above) is the AI ranking which lines carry the most case-relevant content, and highlighting those.
